Hydrogen-Assisted Cold Cracking in High-Strength Steel

Hydrogen-assisted cold cracking requires the combined presence of diffusible hydrogen, a susceptible local microstructure and tensile stress during a critical temperature-time interval. Control measures must address all of these conditions.

Diffusible hydrogen

Moisture in covered electrodes or flux, contaminated wire, damp joint surfaces, coatings, oil and inadequate shielding-gas control can introduce hydrogen to the weld pool. The relevant measure is diffusible hydrogen in deposited weld metal, not total hydrogen. A low-hydrogen consumable classification remains valid in production only when storage, re-drying where applicable, issue time and exposure are controlled.

Microstructural susceptibility

Rapid cooling can produce high-hardness transformation products in the HAZ. Susceptibility depends on composition, delivery condition, prior microstructure and the local weld thermal cycle; carbon equivalent and t8/5 are therefore supporting parameters rather than acceptance criteria. Cracking can also occur in high-strength weld metal, so an HAZ hardness result alone does not establish freedom from hydrogen cracking.

Tensile stress and restraint

Weld shrinkage generates residual tensile stress without external loading. Thick tee and cruciform joints, attachments, closed frames, short repairs and poorly planned sequences can impose high restraint. Root imperfections and abrupt weld profiles add local stress concentration. The restraint in a production detail can therefore be more severe than that in a procedure-test plate.

Delayed formation and inspection

Hydrogen redistribution continues as the joint cools, and cracking can occur after welding is complete. Final NDT performed too early may not reveal delayed cracks. The minimum hold time must be taken from the governing fabrication code or project specification and should reflect material strength, combined thickness, carbon equivalent, heat input and restraint.

Dehydrogenation heat treatment

A controlled post-weld dehydrogenation treatment can maintain the joint at an elevated temperature to promote hydrogen effusion. It is not equivalent to stress-relief post-weld heat treatment. The temperature, holding time and transfer from welding must be qualified for the actual steel. An unqualified generic treatment can temper quenched-and-tempered material and reduce parent-metal or HAZ properties.

Failure investigation

  1. Locate the crack relative to weld metal, fusion boundary and HAZ.
  2. Establish the time of discovery and review consumable batches, issue records and environmental conditions.
  3. Reconstruct actual heat input, preheat, interpass temperature, bead placement and sequence.
  4. Assess hardness, microstructure, fracture morphology and restraint together.
  5. Do not infer the cracking mechanism from elemental analysis alone.
  6. Before repair, remove the causal condition and approve the excavation, preheat and repair-welding procedure.
Hydrogen-assisted cold cracking is controlled by the welding system; no single carbon-equivalent or hardness value demonstrates immunity.
