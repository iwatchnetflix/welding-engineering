Weld Inspection under the Hong Kong Steel Code

Section 14 of the Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Steel 2011 (2023 Edition) defines a fabrication-control route that includes procedure approval, personnel competence, visual inspection, NDT extent, hold time and traceable records. Its numerical requirements should be quoted by clause or table, not replaced by general rules of thumb.

Confirm the governing project documents

Buildings Department building works and CEDD public works do not necessarily use the same contractual requirements. The drawings, specification and inspection and test plan (ITP) should identify the governing Steel Code provisions and any supplementary EN ISO, AWS or project requirements. Where provisions differ, the contract must establish precedence.

Welding procedures and personnel

Steel Code 14.3.3 requires welding procedure specifications to be submitted to the Responsible Engineer before welding starts. Procedure tests are to be witnessed, and the Code uses the term Welding Procedure Approval Record (WPAR). The record includes relevant material certificates, carbon-equivalent information, mechanical-test data and NDT results. Procedure approval and welder or welding-operator qualification are separate controls; neither replaces the other.

Visual inspection

Clause 14.3.6.3 requires visual inspection over the full length of all welds before other required NDT. Welds that will become inaccessible are to be examined before access is lost. Visual inspection addresses dimensions, profile and visible surface imperfections and also establishes whether the surface is suitable for supplementary testing.

Hold time before final NDT

Clause 14.3.6.4 states that a hold time of at least 16 hours should generally be allowed before final inspection of as-welded fabrication. Table 14.2b then varies the typical hold time with IIW carbon equivalent and combined thickness. The Code also calls for reduction for some thin materials below 690 MPa yield strength and increase for combined thickness above 50 mm or yield strength above 690 MPa. High-restraint joints require particular consideration. A universal 24-hour or 48-hour rule is therefore not an accurate statement of the Code.

The selected hold time is measured after completion of welding and is to be stated in the inspection record. Final surface-flaw detection and ultrasonic examination follow the applicable hold time. After repair, the project ITP should define when the hold-time period recommences.

NDT extent for high-strength steel

Table 14.3a defines mandatory NDT by weld type, orientation and thickness. Note 5 states that, for steel with yield strength greater than 500 N/mm², the testing frequency should be 100% unless otherwise agreed by the Responsible Engineer. Any reduction should therefore be a documented project decision supported by the applicable provision and production evidence.

Parent-material ultrasonic testing

Clause 14.3.5 requires, unless otherwise agreed by the Responsible Engineer, ultrasonic inspection before welding of material thicker than 25 mm in tee and cruciform connections within 150 mm of the weld. The inspector reports laminations and significant attenuation variations. This examination addresses parent-material integrity in a susceptible zone; it does not demonstrate through-thickness ductility and does not replace a specified Z-quality requirement.

Traceability and disposition

Material certificate → cut-piece ID → weld ID → WPS/WPAR → welder → parameters → NDT → repair → re-examination → final status
  1. Identify each report by drawing or weld-map location.
  2. Record the welding process, WPS, welder or operator, date and relevant consumable identification.
  3. State the NDT procedure, equipment, calibration, personnel certification, extent and acceptance clause.
  4. Record rejectable indications as nonconformities and apply the specified expansion rules.
  5. Use an approved repair procedure and re-examine the required repaired and adjacent zones.
  6. Close the disposition in the weld map and handover dossier.
Responsible Engineer approval is a project control; it does not remove the need for a stated technical basis, defined scope and traceable record.
