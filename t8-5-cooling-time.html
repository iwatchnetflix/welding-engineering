t8/5 Cooling Time and Heat-Affected-Zone Response

The t8/5 parameter is the time taken for a weld thermal cycle to cool from 800 °C to 500 °C. For many ferritic steels, it is a useful descriptor of the cooling interval in which major austenite transformations occur.

Metallurgical significance

A short t8/5 generally increases the likelihood of hard transformation products; a long t8/5 generally favours softer products and allows more time at elevated temperature. The relationship is steel-specific. Continuous-cooling-transformation data are relevant only when the chemical composition, initial microstructure and austenitising cycle are representative of the product being welded.

Thermal variables

Arc energy or calculated heat input does not uniquely determine t8/5. Heat flow also depends on joint geometry, effective thickness, bead dimensions, preheat, interpass temperature and the position of the pass in a multipass sequence.

VariableTypical effect, subject to joint geometry
Higher arc energy or heat inputUsually lengthens t8/5
Higher preheat or interpass temperatureReduces the temperature gradient and usually slows cooling
Larger effective heat sinkCan shorten t8/5 at the same welding parameters
Start, stop and short-bead regionsCreate transient cycles not represented by steady-state calculations
Multipass weldingReheats existing HAZ and produces intercritically or subcritically reheated regions

Heat-affected-zone sub-regions

  • Coarse-grained HAZ: the peak temperature is sufficiently high for substantial prior-austenite grain growth; toughness can be reduced.
  • Fine-grained HAZ: the region is fully austenitised at a lower peak temperature and generally develops a finer prior-austenite grain size.
  • Intercritical HAZ: only part of the microstructure is austenitised; local softening or brittle constituents can occur depending on the steel and reheating cycle.
  • Subcritical HAZ: no re-austenitisation occurs, but tempering can reduce hardness and strength in quenched-and-tempered steel.

Upper and lower process limits

A lower limit on the qualified thermal input can be required to avoid rapid cooling, excessive hardness and increased susceptibility to hydrogen-assisted cold cracking. An upper limit can be required to control coarse-grained HAZ toughness, HAZ width and softening. The acceptable interval is specific to the product, thickness, joint and property requirements; it is not a generic value attached to “S690” or “S960”.

Use in procedure development

  1. Obtain product-specific t8/5 or heat-input guidance where it is available.
  2. Estimate the cooling time for the actual joint geometry, effective thickness and initial temperature.
  3. Make the procedure test with representative welding parameters, bead size and pass sequence.
  4. Locate hardness, tensile and impact tests so that the relevant HAZ regions are assessed.
  5. Convert the acceptable test conditions into measurable WPS limits for current, voltage, travel speed, bead dimensions, preheat and maximum interpass temperature.
  6. Assess tack welds, attachments, starts, stops and repairs separately where their thermal cycles are more severe.
t8/5 is a thermal-cycle parameter, not a material property. Its allowable range must be supported by the actual product and procedure qualification.
