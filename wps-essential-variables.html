Welding Procedure Qualification: Variables and Range of Qualification

Qualification applies to a defined range of welding conditions. Under ISO 15614-1 the controlling concept is the range of qualification; “essential variables” is the established AWS terminology and should not be imposed on the ISO route without qualification.

Start from the production joint

The review begins with the production material, product form, dimensions, joint, welding process, position and required properties. Those conditions are compared with the recorded test conditions and the range permitted by the governing standard. A company name or certificate cover sheet does not establish coverage.

Variable groups requiring verification

Variable groupTypical items
Parent materialMaterial group and subgroup, product form, grade, delivery condition and dissimilar combination
DimensionsParent-material thickness, deposited weld-metal thickness and outside diameter where applicable
Welding processISO 4063 reference number, process sequence, degree of mechanisation and transfer mode where applicable
Joint and positionButt or fillet weld, penetration, backing, gouging, root technique and welding position
Welding consumablesClassification and designation, diameter, flux-wire combination and shielding or backing gas
Thermal conditionsArc energy or heat input, minimum preheat temperature, maximum interpass temperature and post-weld heat treatment

Qualification level under ISO 15614-1

ISO 15614-1:2017 provides Level 1 and Level 2 procedure tests. Level 2 requires more extensive testing and gives more restrictive qualification ranges. A Level 2 test qualifies Level 1 requirements, but not conversely. If neither the contract nor the application standard specifies a level, ISO 15614-1 requires Level 2.

Thickness is only one qualification dimension

A thicker test piece does not automatically cover every production weld. For example, a change from process 135 to process 136, a change in parent-material group, the addition or removal of backing, or a different process sequence can fall outside the qualified range. The exact effect must be taken from the applicable tables and clauses; it should not be inferred from general welding experience.

Formal range and engineering suitability

Remaining inside a formal qualification range is necessary but may not be sufficient. Impact-test temperature, weld-metal toughness, hardness, hydrogen control, maximum interpass temperature, product-specific heat-input limits and project CTOD requirements can narrow the usable WPS range. This is particularly important for quenched-and-tempered and thermomechanically rolled high-strength steels.

Change-control review

  1. List each production variable and the required property in a controlled comparison sheet.
  2. Record the actual WPQR/PQR test values and results.
  3. Derive the permitted range from the stated code and edition.
  4. Confirm that every production WPS value lies inside that range.
  5. Check any additional restrictions from the execution specification, product manufacturer and design basis.
  6. Refer an out-of-range change for formal disposition before welding; do not treat it as an undocumented shop adjustment.
Qualification range is a code-defined boundary. Suitability is the engineering assessment that the WPS will deliver the required joint properties within that boundary.
