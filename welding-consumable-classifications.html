Reading High-Strength Welding Consumable Classifications

Learn to read solid-wire, flux-cored, submerged-arc and covered-electrode designations without confusing tensile strength, yield strength, test temperature or hydrogen class.

1. A classification is a label with rules

A classification identifies a product against a named standard under specified test conditions. It is different from a manufacturer’s trade name, a batch test result and a qualified welded joint. Read the standard number, edition and classification system before splitting the letters and numbers.

The examples below illustrate how to read the supplied designation strings. They do not certify an unidentified commercial product. Spaces, separators and optional suffixes should be checked against the original package, data sheet and certificate.

2. Choose the right standard and system

Code / itemMeaning and limits
Solid wire / TIG rodISO 16834; Chinese high-strength solid-wire standard: GB/T 39281-2020.
High-strength tubular-cored electrodeISO 18276 / GB/T 36233-2018. ISO 17632 covers non-alloy and fine-grain steels in its specified strength range; GB/T 17493-2018 covers creep-resisting steels.
High-strength SAW wire and wire–flux combinationISO 26304 / GB/T 36034-2018. GB/T 39282-2020 is an EMAT non-destructive testing standard, not a welding-consumable classification.
High-strength covered electrodeISO 18275 / GB/T 32533-2016. Use the edition actually stated on the product documents.
System AStrength classification is based on yield strength, with the 47 J impact framework. Tensile strength and elongation are also specified.
System BStrength classification is based on tensile strength, with the 27 J impact framework and specified supplementary options. It does not omit yield-strength requirements.

Do not call every Chinese standard an identical adoption of System B. For example, the national catalogue records GB/T 36233-2018 and GB/T 32533-2016 as modified adoptions of particular ISO editions. A product can carry more than one classification, but each must be read independently.

3. Solid wire: G76A4M21N5M3

Read the teaching example as G | 76 | A | 4 | M21 | N5M3, with ISO 16834-B and its edition stated separately. The B after the standard number identifies the system; the A inside the designation identifies the test condition.

Code / itemMeaning and limits
GGas-shielded metal arc welding consumable in this standard.
76System-B tensile-strength class. ISO 16834:2025 Table 2 gives 760–960 MPa tensile strength and a minimum yield strength of 680 MPa for 76X; it does not mean an exact measured value of 760 MPa.
AAs-welded classification condition. It does not independently determine whether the structure needs PWHT.
4Impact classification temperature −40 °C. Code 6 corresponds to −60 °C in the relevant table.
M21Shielding-gas group under ISO 14175. 80% Ar / 20% CO₂ is one example; record the actual mixture.
N5M3A chemical-composition designation to be looked up as a whole. N and M indicate nickel/molybdenum-related alloy coding; the digits are not element percentages.

The stated System-B impact framework uses the 27 J level; optional U indicates the supplementary 47 J requirement. Apply the standard’s full result rules.

4. Flux-cored wire: T764T1-1C1A-N4M2

Use the high-strength cored-wire standard. Read the supplied example as T | 76 | 4 | T1 | 1 | C1 | A | N4M2. Confirm the exact national/ISO spelling and any optional suffix on the certificate.

Code / itemMeaning and limits
T / 76 / 4Tubular-cored electrode / System-B tensile-strength class / −40 °C impact temperature.
T1Usability designator: externally gas-shielded, rutile-type core with DC electrode positive under ISO 18276:2024 Table 8.
1Position designator. Check vertical-up versus vertical-down suitability in the applicable edition and supplier instructions; “all-position” is not a WPS or welder qualification.
C1 / ACO₂ shielding / as-welded test condition. A does not mean System A or System B.
N4M2All-weld-metal chemical-composition class; look up all listed elements and limits, rather than treating N4 as 4% Ni.
H5, if declaredMaximum diffusible hydrogen of 5 mL/100 g deposited metal under the specified test and handling conditions. The supplied string has no H5, so it cannot alone prove H5 compliance.

5. SAW: distinguish wire, flux and their combination

A wire has a composition classification; a flux has its own classification; the deposited metal from a specified wire–flux pair has mechanical-property classification. A flux cannot have a tensile strength by itself. One wire with different fluxes may produce different classified results.

Code / itemMeaning and limits
SUGN5M3 — supplied wire labelSU denotes solid SAW wire in System B. SUG is used for an unlisted composition category; G is not simply “high strength”. The appended N5M3 and complete spelling require confirmation from the applicable standard and original manufacturer document.
S78A4UFB — supplied combination fragmentRead as combination information, not a stand-alone flux trade name. S identifies SAW; 78 is a System-B strength-class symbol; A is the tested as-welded condition; 4 is −40 °C; U is a supplementary impact symbol; FB identifies fluoride-basic flux type. Verify the full designation including the wire.
FB versus agglomeratedFB describes flux type/chemistry, not proof of a particular basicity index or manufacturing route. Flux classification under ISO 14174 separately identifies manufacture and other characteristics.

These supplied SAW strings are not presented as an independently verified commercial pair. Obtain the full wire–flux classification, product identities, batch documentation and test condition before procurement or WPS use.

6. Covered electrode: E7815-G

Code / itemMeaning and limits
E / 78Covered electrode / tensile-strength class with a 780 MPa minimum in the relevant B-style classification. Check all remaining mechanical requirements in the stated edition.
15Low-hydrogen sodium-type covering and DC electrode-positive use in this classification family; verify positions and operating restrictions for the product.
-GGeneral/other alloy-composition category with standard-defined conditions and possible purchaser–supplier requirements. It does not mean arbitrary chemistry or automatic interchangeability.

E7815-G alone does not establish an ultra-low hydrogen class, a −60 °C impact guarantee or suitability for every repair. Obtain the hydrogen declaration, complete toughness data, drying instructions and the applicable procedure qualification.

Source: ISO 18275:2026 — covered-electrode classification

7. G89 / G96 and the final selection check

G89 and G96 are incomplete labels. In ISO 16834:2025 System A, 89 and 96 are minimum yield-strength classes of 890 and 960 MPa. They must not be described as System-B tensile classes of 890 and 960 MPa. The full gas, composition, impact and other symbols are still needed.

A higher number does not automatically produce the better joint. Compare specified weld-metal yield and tensile properties with the joint design; confirm impact temperature and energy, hydrogen, gas/flux and tested condition. Then verify coverage by the applicable WPQR/PQR and production WPS. Dedicated cold-cracking tests are added when required; they are not automatically included in every WPQR.

Read in order: standard and edition → system → complete classification → supplier guarantees and batch evidence → joint design → qualification range → production controls.

Standards, versions and further reading

Source check: October 2026. Current ISO publications include 16834:2025, 18276:2024, 26304:2025 and 18275:2026. The cited Chinese standards retain their own edition and adoption relationships. A newer ISO edition does not automatically rewrite a dated national classification or contractual requirement.
