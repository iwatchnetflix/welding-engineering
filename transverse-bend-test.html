Transverse Bend Testing of Structural Steel

A bend test checks whether a specified test piece can withstand plastic deformation under defined conditions. The former, specimen orientation, bend angle and acceptance criteria come from the applicable product standard or purchase specification—not from the expression “transverse bend test” alone.

Read the result in six steps

  • Purpose: assess plastic bending ability under specified conditions; it does not replace impact testing or ultrasonic examination.
  • Transverse sampling: the specimen length is perpendicular to the plate rolling direction.
  • Former: in a common three-point fixture, the central loading tool is the former; the two lower rollers are supports.
  • Dimensions: D is the diameter of the contacting circular former profile; its radius is R = D / 2. For a rounded punch, measure the working nose, not the machine ram or punch shank.
  • Ratios: a is the test-piece thickness; D = 2a and D = 3a are specified diameter-to-thickness ratios, with the smaller diameter usually more demanding under otherwise equal conditions.
  • Endpoint and acceptance: confirm the 180° geometry and the meaning of “sound” in the governing requirement; the result describes the tested specimen.

What the test assesses

In a bend test, a metallic test piece is bent to a specified angle or condition and its tensile surface is examined. The test provides evidence of the material’s ability to accommodate bending strain under the stated test conditions. It may reveal surface-opening imperfections or cracking susceptibility in the tested region, but it is not a general non-destructive examination and does not establish the absence of all internal inclusions, laminations or metallurgical defects.

Impact testing answers a different question: it measures the energy absorbed by a notched specimen under specified impact conditions. A bend test is not a substitute for Charpy impact testing, tensile testing or ultrasonic examination.

What “transverse” means

For a plate specimen, “transverse” normally describes the specimen’s longitudinal axis relative to the rolling direction: the specimen length is perpendicular to the rolling direction. The direction must be stated or read from the relevant product standard and test record; do not infer it from the word “bend” or from the machine setup.

Rolling can produce direction-dependent properties, but the transverse direction is not automatically weaker for every property or every product. A transverse test samples a different orientation and may be specified to assess anisotropic behaviour. Whether it is required, and how the specimen is located and prepared, is product-standard- and order-dependent.

Which part is the former, and what is D?

In the common three-point arrangement, the test piece rests on two lower supports while a central rounded former presses it. The former is the upper contacting tool; the two lower cylinders are supports. D is the diameter of the former—not the diameter of the supports and not a radius. ISO 7438 also permits other test arrangements, so the exact fixture must follow the method invoked.

D = former diameter    |    a = test-piece thickness

When a specification gives D = 2a or D = 3a, the former diameter is respectively two or three times the test-piece thickness a. For a full-thickness 10 mm plate specimen, those specified ratios would mean D = 20 mm or D = 30 mm. This is an arithmetic example only; it does not establish which ratio applies to a particular steel grade.

For the same specimen thickness and setup, a smaller D gives a tighter bend and generally increases tensile strain at the outer surface. It is therefore usually the more demanding condition. Severity is not determined by D alone: specimen dimensions and preparation, bend direction, supports, angle, test procedure and acceptance rule also matter.

What does a 180° bend require?

“180°” specifies the target bend angle, but it does not by itself define every detail of the final geometry. Depending on the applicable product requirement, the specimen’s sides may be brought into contact or held parallel at a specified separation. Springback and the specified testing route also matter. The method and endpoint should therefore be taken from the product standard or order rather than described simply as “press until the ends touch”.

How is “sound” or “完好” judged?

Inspect the tensile (outer/convex) surface after bending and record any cracks or other indications. The acceptance criterion—including what size, type or location of imperfection is permitted—must come from the governing product standard, specification or agreed test requirement. “No visible crack or delamination” should not be presented as a universal ISO 7438 acceptance rule unless the controlling document says so. A bend test also cannot certify that the whole plate is free from internal defects.

How this relates to S690QL and the European standards

EN ISO 7438:2020 specifies the bend-test method. It states that the former diameter is determined by the relevant product standard. It does not make D = 2a, D = 3a, a 180° target or a particular “sound” criterion universal for every steel.

For S690QL flat products, EN 10025-6:2019+A1:2022 is the product standard. Its informative Annex B gives recommended inside bend radii for flanging; a forming radius for a manufacturing operation is not automatically the former diameter or acceptance condition for a laboratory bend test. Check the edition, product form, thickness, order options and any agreed supplementary requirement before specifying a test. The grade name S690QL alone does not show that a plate must pass both D = 2a and D = 3a.

Parent material is not a welded joint

A transverse bend test on plate samples the parent material. Bend tests on welded-joint procedure test specimens—such as face, root or side bends—assess the welded joint and are covered by EN ISO 5173:2023. The specimen layout, former, test orientation and acceptance criteria may differ. Do not use a parent-plate result as a substitute for the required WPQR bend tests, or vice versa.

Practical reading: identify the specimen axis and product standard first; confirm what the symbol a refers to; read D as the former’s diameter; then verify the angle, fixture and acceptance rule in the governing requirement. Treat D = 2a and D = 3a as specified test conditions—not universal properties of Q690qE or S690QL.
