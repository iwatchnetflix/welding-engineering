Bend Testing Explained: Plate and Welded Joints

A beginner’s guide to the specimen, former diameter, test procedure and acceptance evidence—with separate routes for plate tests and PQR / WPQR weld qualification.

1. What is a bend test?

Take a strip of metal, bend it around a rounded tool under specified conditions, and inspect the stretched outside surface. A bend test asks: can this specimen accommodate the required plastic deformation without unacceptable opening imperfections? It is a destructive test: the tested strip is permanently deformed and is not returned to the structure.

Imagine folding a thick metal strip into a U: the inner surface is compressed and the outer surface is stretched. An internal imperfection intersecting the tested region may open during this deformation. Passing demonstrates performance under the recorded conditions; it does not prove that the entire plate or every production weld is defect-free.

2. First distinguish plate tests from weld tests

TestWhat is sampled?What does transverse mean?Engineering question
Parent-plate bend testAn unwelded plate stripSpecimen length perpendicular to rolling directionDoes the supplied material meet the specified bend requirement?
Welded-joint bend testA strip containing weld metal, heat-affected zones and parent metalSpecimen length perpendicular to the weld axis for a transverse weld specimenDoes the test weld meet the qualification requirement?

The two reference directions are different. In a weld bend test, face, root and side describe the surface placed in tension, not the rolling direction. Rolling can cause anisotropy, but transverse properties are not universally lower for every steel or every property.

Weld test typeSurface on the outside of the bendRegion examined
Face bendThe original weld face / cap sideFace-side weld and adjacent zones
Root bendThe original weld root sideRoot region and adjacent zones
Side bendA machined side section through the weld thicknessThe weld section through the original thickness

A side-bend strip cut from a thick plate can have a bending thickness of 10 mm while its width spans the original plate thickness. Therefore the original plate thickness and the bend-specimen thickness are not always the same dimension.

For the cited ISO 15614-1:2017 butt-joint route, thickness below 12 mm uses two root and two face bends; at 12 mm or above, four side bends may replace them. Other codes use different thresholds: the cited GB 50661-2011 route uses 14 mm. Do not transfer specimen counts or thickness boundaries between codes.

3. Locate the former and read the parameters

In the common three-point arrangement, the central upper tool pushes the specimen down. Its rounded contacting end is the former. The two lower rollers are supports. D refers to the working circular profile diameter of the former, not the support diameter, machine ram or tool shank. A rounded punch need not be a complete cylinder.

Symbol / parameterMeaningHow to read it
DFormer diameter, mm; radius R = D / 2D = 40 mm means a 20 mm radius

D/aFormer-diameter to specimen-thickness ratioD = 4a and a = 10 mm give D = 40 mm

This article uses D and a for clarity. ISO 5173:2023 uses d for the former and tₛ for specimen thickness; its capital D denotes pipe outside diameter. Always read the symbol definitions in the actual report. A or A₅ also requires the specified tensile gauge length: do not substitute a value measured with a different gauge length or a typical catalogue value.

For equal specimen thickness, angle and setup, a smaller former gives a tighter bend and normally greater outer-surface strain. A larger former makes the test less severe. The specified diameter must therefore be selected before testing and recorded; changing it to obtain a pass changes the test condition.

4. How the test is performed

  1. Identify the controlling standard, edition, qualification level and any application requirements. Specify test type, number, dimensions, former, angle and acceptance criteria before cutting specimens.
  2. Cut and identify specimens from the prescribed locations. Remove weld reinforcement or backing where required, dress cutting damage and prepare edges to the specified radius without removing genuine indications.
  3. Place the correct face on the tension side and position the weld in the intended bend region. Apply the specified plunger, roller or wrap-around method under controlled loading conditions.
  4. Reach the prescribed endpoint and inspect the convex surface. Measure indications, locate them in weld metal, fusion boundary, HAZ or parent metal, and photograph the relevant surfaces.
  5. Compare every required specimen with the selected acceptance clauses. Record pass, fail or an invalid test/retest disposition with its reason.

A 180° bend does not automatically mean the ends touch. The final gap and any subsequent flattening depend on the method and requirement. “Cold” usually refers to bending without deliberate hot forming under the specified test conditions; it does not mean a low-temperature impact test.

5. How is the former diameter selected?

Plate: read the product requirement

For an unwelded plate, the test method such as ISO 7438 / GB/T 232 describes how to bend; the steel product standard or purchase requirement supplies the required diameter, angle and acceptance condition. D = 2a or D = 3a may apply to particular products or thickness ranges. Their appearance in a table does not by itself prove that every specimen must pass both conditions.

Weld qualification: apply the chosen code route

Under the conventional ISO 5173 steel route, parent metal with specified minimum A ≥ 20% uses a former diameter of 4a. When A < 20%, the elongation-based relationship is D = (100 × a / A) − a. Confirm the invoked edition and application provisions, including dissimilar materials and material-specific exceptions. This is not one universal formula for ISO, AWS, ASME and every GB qualification code.

D = (100 × a / A) − a   when the invoked rule requires it

The 14% example is hypothetical, not a declared requirement for every S690QL product. Check grade, thickness, delivery condition and the governing specification for the required A. Select available tooling only within the permitted dimensional tolerance or documented rule. A larger tool lowers strain; a smaller tool raises it. Neither direction of rounding is automatically acceptable.

The geometric idea can be estimated as outer-surface strain ε ≈ a / (D + a), assuming the neutral axis remains near mid-thickness. Thus 4a corresponds to about 20% and 6a to about 14.3%. This idealised estimate explains the trend; it is not a tensile elongation measurement, a fracture prediction or permission to override the prescribed fixture.

AWS D1.1 uses its own guided-bend jig dimensions and material-specific notes. Read the chosen edition’s fixture figures and notes; do not reduce selection to “yield strength ≤345 MPa means 4a, otherwise 6a”. The 2025 contents identify the guided-bend fixture figures as 6.11–6.13. A table or annex number copied from another edition is not a reliable instruction.

6. What is measured, and what counts as a pass?

Routine qualification bends usually report conformity rather than a bend-strength value in MPa. The evidence still contains measured quantities: specimen dimensions, actual former diameter, bend angle and each indication’s largest dimension, location and, where required, cumulative length. A pass box without this evidence is difficult to audit.

Controlling requirementAcceptance exampleImportant boundary

GB 50661-2011, 6.5.3 and 6.5.4Butt-joint example: D = 4a; 180°; each indication ≤3 mm; per-specimen total ≤7 mm; four-specimen total ≤24 mmIts fixed diameter and cumulative limits must not be replaced by another code’s formula or 10 mm limit

Corner cracks are not universally exempt. Their treatment can depend on cause, length and replacement-test provisions. Keep photographs and investigate whether a crack follows a fusion defect, inclusion or a machining notch. Apply the actual code disposition; do not explain away a weld defect simply because it reaches an edge.

7. What does this achieve in engineering?

For plate procurement, a bend result supports acceptance of the supplied material under the specified conditions. For procedure qualification, it is one part of the evidence that the tested welding procedure produces an acceptable joint. Face, root and side bends expose different regions to tensile deformation and can reveal opening imperfections that a strength result alone may miss.

A PQR / WPQR records actual welding variables and required test results; a production WPS is supported within the resulting qualification range. Passing the bend portion alone does not complete the qualification. Tensile, impact, hardness, macro examination and NDT may also be required.

Do not use a bend pass to claim low-temperature toughness, fatigue life, fracture toughness, design bending capacity or permission to cold-form the production member to the same radius. Those are different engineering questions requiring their own evidence.

Use the edition and amendments required by the contract, including any dated normative references. A newly published method standard does not automatically replace a dated reference in an existing qualification code. The numerical examples here are teaching examples, not project results or steel-grade guarantees.

A practical report-review checklist

Standard and edition → material and specified A → specimen type and actual a → former D and fixture → angle → indication sizes and locations → acceptance clause → all-specimen conclusion and qualification range.

A useful result line includes specimen ID, face/root/side type, a, D, angle, indication dimensions and locations, clause reference and disposition. “Pass” is the conclusion of this chain, not a substitute for it.
