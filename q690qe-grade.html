Q690qE: Reading the Grade Designation Correctly

Q690qE is a bridge structural steel grade in GB/T 714-2025. Its name identifies a strength class, bridge-steel designation and impact-quality grade, but it does not by itself specify every property needed for procurement, design or welding.

The four parts of the designation

SymbolMeaning
QInitial letter of qu (屈), indicating yield strength
690Nominal yield-strength level in MPa. GB/T 714-2025 specifies a minimum of 690 MPa for nominal thickness up to 50 mm and 650 MPa for thickness over 50 mm up to 100 mm.
qInitial letter of qiao (桥), identifying bridge steel under GB/T 714
EQuality grade with Charpy V-notch impact testing at −40°C

For Q690q, the quality grades listed in GB/T 714-2025 are D, E and F, corresponding to Charpy test temperatures of −20°C, −40°C and −60°C respectively. Grade C is used for some lower-strength bridge steels in the same standard, but it is not a Q690q quality grade.

Concise definition: Q690qE is a Q690-strength-class bridge structural steel to GB/T 714-2025. The lowercase q identifies bridge steel and E identifies the −40°C Charpy V-notch quality grade. The specified minimum yield strength remains thickness-dependent.

Delivery condition: read the certificate

The delivery route is not encoded in the name Q690qE. GB/T 714-2025 permits thermomechanical rolling (TMCP), TMCP followed by tempering, or quenching and tempering, with the actual delivery condition stated on the quality certificate. Describing all Q690qE as “TMCP + tempered” is therefore too restrictive. Welding decisions must use the delivered product and certificate, not the grade name alone.

Base-grade requirements and supplementary requirements

The base standard controls matters including chemical composition, weldability indicators, tensile properties and impact properties. Other requirements must be identified separately in the order, drawings and project specification.

RequirementCorrect interpretation
Through-thickness propertiesNot inherent in Q690qE. Where required, a Z15, Z25 or Z35 suffix is added and the corresponding requirements of GB/T 5313 apply.
Fatigue performanceNot established by the grade name. Fatigue resistance depends strongly on the structural detail, weld geometry, imperfection level, residual stress, fabrication quality and applicable design rules.
Parent-plate ultrasonic testingAny required class, extent and acceptance criteria must be stated in the procurement or project documents.
Yield ratio or tighter property limitsUse the requirements of the invoked edition plus any explicitly agreed supplementary limits; do not infer them from q alone.

Why Q690qE is not simply “Q690E for bridges”

A material designation is meaningful only together with its product standard. The shorthand Q690E may refer to a product supplied under a different Chinese standard, with different chemistry, delivery conditions, thickness ranges and test requirements. Calling it “engineering-machinery steel” is not a sufficiently precise technical definition. Q690qE and a Q690E product must not be treated as interchangeable unless the governing design and material requirements have been compared and substitution has been formally accepted.

Comparison with S690QL

S690QL is the more direct EN comparison when the purpose is to explain the impact-quality suffix: Q690qE and S690QL both specify Charpy impact properties at −40°C. S690QL1 instead specifies impact properties at −60°C.

The shared number 690 does not make the grades equivalent. Strength, toughness, chemical composition, delivery condition, dimensions, inspection documentation, execution rules and design assumptions all require comparison.

Welding implications

Q690qE should not be labelled simply as having “high cold-cracking sensitivity” without the actual product data and joint conditions. Hydrogen-assisted cracking risk depends on composition and delivered microstructure together with plate thickness, heat input, preheat and interpass temperature, diffusible hydrogen, joint restraint and weld-metal strength. A defensible welding review should therefore:

  1. confirm the standard edition, full grade, dimensions, delivery condition and inspection certificate;
  2. use the applicable CEV or Pcm information for the delivered product;
  3. define consumable hydrogen control, joint restraint and the thermal window;
  4. qualify the material group, thickness, joint and welding sequence under the governing procedure standard; and
  5. verify all required tensile, impact, hardness and NDT results before transferring limits to production.
A grade name is the starting point for material identification, not a substitute for the certificate, purchase specification, design rules or qualified welding procedure.
