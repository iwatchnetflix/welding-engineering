Hardness Testing of Welded Joints

A hardness traverse maps local response across a welded joint. It is meaningful only when the indentation positions, test load and governing acceptance requirement are stated; an isolated value such as 350 HV is not a universal limit.

What hardness reveals

Vickers hardness correlates broadly with local strength and microstructure. A sharp HAZ peak can indicate fast cooling and a hard transformation product; a trough can reveal tempering or softening in quenched-and-tempered steel. Hardness is therefore useful in procedure qualification and failure investigation, but it is not a direct measurement of toughness, ductility or hydrogen content.

Test method matters

ISO 9015-1 covers hardness testing on cross-sections of arc-welded joints, commonly using HV5 or HV10. The load, surface preparation, indentation spacing, test-line position and distance from surfaces must be reported. Results from different loads or layouts should not be compared as if they were identical.

Where to place indentations

  • Use sufficient points in parent metal, HAZ and weld metal to show the profile.
  • Place HAZ indentations close enough to capture the narrow peak or softened region without violating minimum spacing rules.
  • For multipass welds, consider cap, mid-thickness and root traverses because reheating differs.
  • For dissimilar joints, sample both parent steels and both HAZs.
  • Record a macrograph with the traverse locations; a list of numbers without location has limited value.

Why a high value can matter

High local hardness may indicate a microstructure more susceptible to hydrogen-assisted cracking, particularly when diffusible hydrogen and tensile stress are present. The relationship depends on steel type and service environment. A hardness value does not cause cracking on its own, and a value below a remembered threshold does not prove immunity.

Why a low value can matter

In Q&T and some TMCP steels, welding can create a softened HAZ. If the soft zone is wide and highly stressed, it can affect joint strength or deformation. A maximum-hardness criterion alone misses this issue; the reviewer should consider minimum hardness, zone width, transverse tensile behaviour and structural demand.

Where the acceptance value comes from

The application standard, material group, service environment and project specification define acceptance. Limits used for sour service, pressure equipment or a particular procedure qualification cannot automatically be imported into a building or bridge project. ISO 9015-1 tells the laboratory how to test; it does not create one universal pass/fail value for every material.

A useful report contains

  1. Material identity, thickness, delivery condition and weld procedure.
  2. Test method, load, equipment and calibration status.
  3. Macrograph with every traverse and indentation location.
  4. Individual readings—not only a maximum.
  5. Applicable acceptance clause and any project modification.
  6. Comment on both hard peaks and softened zones.
The correct question is not “Is it below 350 HV?” but “What limit applies to this material, service and qualification route—and did the traverse sample the critical microstructure?”
