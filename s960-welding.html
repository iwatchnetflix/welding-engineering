Welding S960: Defining and Qualifying the Thermal Window

S960 identifies a nominal strength level, not a complete material specification. Welding requirements must be based on the product standard, grade suffix, delivery condition, thickness, joint configuration and required joint properties.

Product identification

S960 products are available under different product standards and delivery routes. For example, quenched-and-tempered plate grades under EN 10025-6 and thermomechanically rolled cold-forming grades under EN 10149-2 are not metallurgically interchangeable. The inspection document and manufacturer’s product data must therefore be reviewed before a welding procedure is selected.

Lower thermal limit

Low arc energy, a large heat sink and low initial temperature can shorten t8/5 and produce high-hardness HAZ microstructures. Hydrogen-assisted cold-cracking susceptibility then depends on diffusible hydrogen and restraint as well as cooling rate. The lower procedural limit is therefore established from product composition, combined thickness, joint geometry, preheat, consumable hydrogen level and restraint.

Upper thermal limit

High arc energy or excessive interpass temperature can lengthen the thermal cycle, widen the HAZ and increase grain growth or tempering. In quenched-and-tempered steel, intercritical and subcritical HAZ regions may soften relative to the delivered plate. The structural significance depends on the soft-zone width, joint geometry, weld-metal mismatch and stress state; a hardness minimum or tensile test may be needed in addition to a maximum-hardness check.

Procedure-development sequence

  1. Verify the exact product designation, delivery condition, thickness range and inspection document.
  2. Review product-manufacturer limits and the project’s EN/ISO or other governing requirements.
  3. Select compatible low-hydrogen welding consumables with appropriate strength and impact properties at the design temperature.
  4. Set preliminary limits for preheat, maximum interpass temperature, arc energy or heat input, bead dimensions and sequence.
  5. Qualify the actual material combination, joint type, thickness and process sequence.
  6. Assess transverse tensile strength and fracture location, impact properties where specified, hardness distribution and HAZ softening.
  7. Transfer only the supported range to the production WPS and verify that production can monitor it.

Fanling North application

Public information from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University describes the development of S960 box-girder welding technology using testing and numerical simulation for different plate thicknesses and joint designs, followed by application to factory-fabricated footbridge girders. The transferable lesson is the qualification approach, not a single heat-input value: product, joint geometry, thermal cycle and measured joint performance were considered together.

The inspection document establishes the delivered plate properties. Procedure qualification establishes evidence for the welded joint within a defined range.
